feat(3d): real 3-thread fleece construction (face + binding + fleecy float)

Fleece was never actually modeled as fleece in the 3D view — it fell into
the same generic single-bed branch as plain single jersey, with the
brushed nap bolted on afterward as a decorative instanced-fibre layer with
no structural relationship to the knit. Given a real 3-thread fleece
construction diagram (needle loop, sinker loop, binding/tuck yarn, fleecy
yarn, float), rebuilt it properly, scoped to fleece only:

- topology-builder.js: a real second yarn course per row — the binding+
  fleecy yarn — alongside the plain-knit face course. It ties in (tuck,
  reusing the existing tuckCup anatomy) every FLEECE_TUCK_EVERY-th wale
  (the textbook 3-thread ratio) and floats the rest of the way (reusing
  missFloat), staggered course to course like a real tie-in pattern. The
  float segments are exported as world-space anchors.
- knit-renderer.js: those anchors are handed to the pile builder, so nap
  fibres root at the real float positions instead of a uniform random
  scatter with no connection to the knit. The flat opaque "backing board"
  every other construction needs (to hide see-through gaps) is skipped for
  fleece — the real floats plus the nap now give the back genuine
  structural coverage, which a real fabric has anyway; the flat board was
  exactly the slab artefact flagged against real photos.
- pile.js: the brushed-fibre geometry itself was wrong at any zoom close
  enough to actually see it — 0.55x-radius-wide, 4.2x-radius-long 5-facet
  cones read as rigid angular blades, not soft fuzz (this is what the
  "rows of trees" artefact in the screenshots actually was, now confirmed
  by rendering it in isolation). Thinned and shortened to read as fine
  teased-out filament instead of a scaled-down copy of the yarn it came
  from, with one more radial facet for a rounder silhouette.

Verified visually against production's real engine output for
fleece_3_thread: technical face renders a plain-knit structure (correct —
that is what a real 3-thread fleece face is); technical back now shows
fibre clusters rooted at real tuck/float positions with no flat panel
behind them, not the previous uniform scatter of oversized blades.
Density is deliberately conservative — pushing it further caused the
swatch's build cost to spike badly enough to stall the page in headless
testing, and a real device deserves the same caution. Left as a known
next step if a fuller nap is wanted.

Scoped to fleece only, as asked; every other construction's code path is
untouched.
