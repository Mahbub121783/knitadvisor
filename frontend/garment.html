1 Fabric

The chart is the finished, flat, after-wash garment. The shrinkage is how much this cloth shrinks in the wash: the pieces are cut that much larger. The wales and courses only draw the knit on the 3D garment.

2 Measurement chart

Flat widths, as a spec sheet gives them. * The chest and the length are needed; anything else left empty is taken from the chest, and the chart says so. The marker ratio is how many garments of each size are cut together.

A front opening cuts the front in two halves with an allowance and a facing; a quarter zip leaves the front whole and adds a placket over a slit. The length is bought in the steps a catalogue sells.

Building the patterns…
Seams, stitches and thread

The stitch type decides the machine and how much thread a seam takes (rules of thumb: thread length per length of seam); the allowance of a seam is what is added to its edges when the piece is cut. A hem has no allowance: its depth is in the drawing.
