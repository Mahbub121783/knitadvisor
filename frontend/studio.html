Fabric Studio

Design a new weft-knitted fabric needle by needle. Pick the machine type, the yarns and the target, draw the knit, tuck and miss programme, and the studio checks it, sets out the cams and needles, works out the yarn on every feeder and shows the fabric in 3D. Every figure is tagged measured from factory records, calculated from your inputs, or estimated.

    Knitting the swatch…

    Each tube is one yarn; each course is one feeder's pass. A knit loop is drawn through the loop below it, back to front. A tuck is an open arch with no crossing at its foot, lying behind the held loop. A miss is a straight float behind the held loop. A held loop reaches up to the course where its needle next knits. Point at a yarn to read the stitch; click a feeder below to pick out its yarn. Drag to turn, scroll to zoom. Loop shape follows the finished wales and courses per cm; yarn thickness follows each feeder's count.

    The real view is the same 3D engine as the fabric calculator: real yarn (twist, hairiness, fibre sheen), lighting and cloth surface, knitted from exactly the programme you drew, at the fabric's own wales and courses per cm, yarn count and GSM. It redraws on every change. It opens on the loops so every K, T and M you drew can be read in real yarn; 4 cm and Cloth step back to the swatch and the whole piece. Drag to turn, scroll to zoom.

    Needle notation, one line per feeder, feeder 1 at the top. Dots are needles (dial on top, cylinder below on a double bed). A closed loop round a needle is a knit, an open arch a tuck, a straight line a miss.

    Knitting cycle — one needle of your programme
    Feeder Needle

      The needle you pick in your programme, seen in section, through the eight positions of the latch-needle cycle. Knit, tuck, miss and transfer each follow their own cam path; the loops already on the needle come from the cells above it in your notation. On a double bed (rib, interlock) there is no sinker: each bed knocks over across the edge of its trick and the other bed holds the fabric. Use the arrows, or click a position, to stop at it.

      All needles past the cam box
      Bed

      The needles run past the cam box from left to right: each feeder's cam lifts a needle to clearing height (knit), to tuck height, or leaves it down (miss), and the stitch cam then draws it down to knock over. The rings on each stem are the loops that needle is holding — watch a tuck add one and a knit clear them.

      Needle types
      1 Structure

      Each guide bar has its own warp ends. Write its lapping course by course: 1-0/1-2// is tricot, 1-0/2-3// a 2-needle underlap, 1-0/0-1// pillar, 0-0/2-2// an inlay. Bar 1 is the front bar. On a double-needle-bar machine the pairs alternate front bed, back bed (1-0/1-1// = a loop on the front bed only; 1-0/0-1// = a spacer yarn looping on both), or write a course as four numbers, 1-0-0-1.
